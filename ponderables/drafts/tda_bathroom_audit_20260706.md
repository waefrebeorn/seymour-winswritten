## ISSUE 1 — "The Penny Motif" (bathroom_pennies.md)

**THE HOOK:** Mom darted into traffic for pennies. Wife finds them heads-up. He built a 5-persona taxonomy to analyze why he picks up change.

**PASS 1 — IS THE FACT REAL?**
- ✅ "Penny costs 2.7¢ to make / Mint loses $85M/yr" — directionally true. (US Mint's 2024 per-unit cost is ~3.07¢; 2.7¢ was the older figure. Close enough for a toilet book, but bump it to "about 3 cents" to be safe.)
- ✅ "Canada killed theirs in 2012" — correct (eliminated Feb 2013, announced 2012).
- ✅ "96 trillion pennies = $960B" — arithmetic checks: 96T × $0.01 = $960B. Correct.
- ⚠️ "Heads up = good luck, tails up = leave it" — folk rule, fine, but it's *his* family rule presented as universal. Flag as personal lore, not fact.

**PASS 2 — DOES THE STRUCTURE HOLD?**
- The 5-persona taxonomy (Colonel / Clipart / Devil / Philosopher / Engineer) is the series spine. Presenting it *inside* the penny entry is smart — it shows the method, not just the result. Structure holds.
- Risk: mentions "Taco Bell dogs, old batteries, 96 trillion pennies and a shitty truck" as transcript mess. Cute, but unverified color. If those aren't in the transcripts, it's invented texture. **Verify before print.**

**PASS 3 — WHAT'S THE TRAP?**
- Trap: the entry is *so* self-aware ("he knows it's ridiculous, that's the bit") that it pre-empts criticism and becomes unfalsifiable. A devil says: if every weakness is confessed first, is there anything left to audit? The kicker lands anyway. Keep the confession — it's the voice — but don't let it defang the analysis.

**VERDICT:** 🟢 PUBLISH WITH FIXES — correct the 2.7¢→~3¢ and verify the Taco Bell line.

## ISSUE 3 — "The Man Who Read Hatchet" (bathroom_paulsen.md)

- ❌ **"Paulsen ran the Iditarod. Twice."** — FALSE. He raced the **Iditarod once** (1983, finished 41st). This is a hard factual error. Fix to "once" or cut the detail.

**PASS 2 — DOES THE STRUCTURE HOLD?**

**PASS 3 — WHAT'S THE TRAP?**

## ISSUE 5 — "The Guy Who Writes CUDA Kernels" (bathroom_cuda.md)

**THE HOOK:** He writes GPU inference engines for fun, debugs bank conflicts at 2 AM, bans chat's "just use cuBLAS."

**PASS 1 — IS THE FACT REAL?**
- ❌ **"A single H100 does 67 TFLOPS FP16."** — WRONG. H100 FP16 is ~494 TFLOPS (non-sparse) / ~990 (sparse). 67 TFLOPS is roughly **H100 FP64** or an A100-class figure. This is a factual miss that a GPU person will spot instantly. Fix to "~990 TFLOPS FP16 (sparse)" or "~494 (dense)."
- ✅ "RTX 4050 laptop ~12 TFLOPS FP16" — plausible (~12–15). OK.
- ✅ "Q6_K = 6-bit, 32 at a time, edge cases when not multiple of 32" — accurate to GGML Q6_K packing. Correct.
- ✅ "Gemma 4 12B at 18 tok/s on laptop GPU" — consistent with project notes. OK.

**PASS 2 — DOES THE STRUCTURE HOLD?**
- "The code is the point, the struggle is the product" is the real thesis and it's earned. Holds.

**PASS 3 — WHAT'S THE TRAP?**
- Trap: tech specs in a toilet book risk becoming a spec sheet (slop). The H100 error proves the danger — get the number right OR cut it for the "welds metal" type weird fact. For a bathroom reader, the weird fact wins.

**VERDICT:** 🔴 FIX BEFORE PUBLISH — correct the H100 TFLOPS figure.
