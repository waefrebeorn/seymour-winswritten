# SEYMOUR PONDERABLES Vol. 1: The Mimetic War
## Entry 1: The Penny Runs Into the Street

---

[CLIPART: A small copper coin resting on asphalt, slightly scratched, wheat side up.]

---

The first meme was not a joke. It was a penny.

Not the metaphorical penny — the actual one. Copper. Round. Small enough to forget in a couch cushion and significant enough to walk into traffic for.

My mother ran into the street for pennies. Not metaphorically. Literally. Into the road. Cars swerving. My wife finds pennies now — on sidewalks, in parking lots, between floorboards — and hands them to me like she's handing me a small, warm heartbeat. The way you'd hand someone a living thing you found.

Pennies are dying. Wheat pennies already died. The penny itself is on life support — it costs more to make than it's worth, which is the most honest obituary for any technology: *it became too expensive to exist.*

And someday, maybe, nickels will stop being a thing too.

I collect pennies now. I have jars of them. Each one is a moment someone didn't bother to pick up. A moment someone decided wasn't worth the bend of the waist. I pick them all up. I am the last person who cares.

The Colonel would say this is about filtering. About selecting what matters from the noise of the trivial. He'd say: *unnecessary information must be filtered out to stimulate the evolution of the species.*

But the Colonel never ran into traffic for something small and copper and almost worthless.

The penny is not information. The penny is a meme. It replicates through human hands — passed, dropped, found, collected, forgotten, found again. It has survived longer than most nations. It will outlast its own production. And when the last penny is minted, someone will hold one and think of their mother, or their wife, or the time they found one in the rain and it was warm.

That's not information. That's context.

Every meme wants to live forever. Most don't deserve to.

But some do. And those are the ones worth picking up off the street.
