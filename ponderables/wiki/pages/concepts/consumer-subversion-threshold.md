# Consumer Subversion Threshold

## Overview

The threshold at which consumers engage in gray-market imports or regional arbitrage to bypass tariffs — analyzed through the Nintendo Switch 2 case study.

## Factors Limiting Subversion

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Logistics** | Shipping costs, customs complexity, delivery time |
| **Warranties** | Manufacturer warranties often region-locked or voided |
| **Region-free software** | Nintendo's region-free policy mitigates but doesn't eliminate |
| **Cultural pull** | Strong brand loyalty reduces incentive to seek alternatives |
| **Price sensitivity** | $450 console — tariff impact absorbed in broader inflation context |

## Switch 2 Evidence

- **No massive gray-market subversion at scale** observed
- Determined buyers found workarounds but marginal volume
- Tariffs "pulled some demand forward" (pre-order acceleration) without category collapse
- Broader inflation on necessities posed greater consumer risk than gaming-specific hits

## Theoretical Threshold

Subversion scales when:
1. Price delta > logistics + risk + warranty loss
2. Product is commodity (low differentiation)
3. Reliable supply channels exist (established importers, regional stock)

Nintendo's cultural cachet and region-free software raised the threshold.
