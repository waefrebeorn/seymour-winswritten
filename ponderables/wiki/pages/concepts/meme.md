# Meme

A meme is a unit of cultural transmission, coined by Richard Dawkins in *The Selfish Gene* (1976). Like genes for biology, memes compete for limited attention and memory — replicating through imitation, mutating through misremembering, selected by their environment (culture, platforms, trends).

## In the Ponderables

Seymour treats memes as the central subject: not internet jokes, but the fundamental building blocks of culture. Every entry in the series traces how a particular meme (or memeplex) emerged, spread, mutated, and either died or became permanent.

## Key Properties

- **Replication** — memes spread through copying, not instruction
- **Mutation** — every retelling introduces variation
- **Selection** — environment determines which memes survive (Kubrickian jungle metaphor)
- **Fidelity** — some memes replicate perfectly (catchphrases), others mutate constantly (video formats)
