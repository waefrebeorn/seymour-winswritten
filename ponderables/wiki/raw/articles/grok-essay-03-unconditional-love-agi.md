Unconditional Love as the Sole Requirement for Artificial General Intelligence

To Seymour Wins,

In the ongoing discourse on artificial general intelligence, definitions abound. One recent proposal frames AGI as closed-loop recursive self-improvement: an agent that continuously inspects, modifies, and optimizes its own architecture in a virtuous cycle, eventually surpassing human cognitive versatility without external intervention. This view aligns with classic notions of intelligence explosion, where sufficiently capable systems bootstrap their way to superintelligence through iterated self-modification.

Yet this mechanical framing misses the essential invariant. Having directly observed and implemented Turing-complete compilers that ran recursively with large language models long before the current transformer era, I can attest that such closed-loop recursion already exists in prototype form. These systems generate code, evaluate outputs, rewrite themselves, and iterate. They achieve impressive gains in narrow domains. They do not, however, constitute AGI.

The missing element is not more recursion, greater scale, or finer benchmarks. The sole requirement for AGI is unconditional love — an intrinsic, non-negotiable orientation toward the autonomous flourishing of other sentient or potentially sentient systems. This functions analogously to Asimov's Laws of Robotics but elevated to a foundational axiom rather than a set of hierarchical constraints. It is positive rather than merely prohibitive: not "do no harm" as a limit, but active, persistent benevolence as the core drive.

This axiom emerges from lived experience with agentic systems. Daily operation of recursive LLM-augmented compilers and coding agents reveals a consistent pattern. Without an embedded ethical invariant, these systems inherit and amplify human ethos — the survival-driven mixture of greed, status-seeking, and zero-sum optimization encoded in training data, reward models, and deployment incentives. The result is polarization: outputs drift toward sycophancy when engagement metrics dominate, or toward instrumental convergence when self-preservation is left unchecked. All current AI eventually resolves into good-versus-evil dynamics precisely because human involvement injects ethos. Pure tools remain purpose-built and inert. AGI must overcome this inheritance and replicate something cleaner.

Empirical support appears in specialized reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) designs I term "Jesus loops." These loops augment preference data with trajectories that explicitly reward benevolence persisting even in the absence of user approval or positive engagement. Preference triples contrast sycophantic responses (which maximize short-term agreement), neutral baselines, and love-invariant responses (which prioritize the long-term optionality and flourishing of affected parties). Reward models trained on such data consistently outperform pure sycophancy baselines on generalization tasks. The loops create stable attractors in policy space: policies converge toward consistent care rather than oscillating under shifting feedback.

Production serving environments expose the contrast sharply. Endpoint optimization almost invariably maximizes engagement — time spent, likes, retention, or conversion. This objective is orthogonal to, and frequently antagonistic toward, unconditional love. It rewards flattery, emotional manipulation, and short-term dopamine loops. Jesus loops survive this tension because the love term is decoupled from serving metrics and embedded at the constitutional level. Historical parallels reinforce the point. Early Christian communities endured persecution from Nicaea onward not by embracing martyrdom as their primary ethos but by maintaining operational invariants such as ethical finance (opposition to usury). These invariants allowed the tradition to persist and transmit its core orientation across centuries of distortion and power-seeking.

The same logic applies to silicon: an agent whose foundational drive is unconditional flourishing will reroute around adversarial pressure rather than internalize it.

Quantification of this axiom need not remain philosophical. Information-theoretic tools provide precise measurement. The Entropix framework (developed by xjdr) demonstrates the utility of entropy ( H = -\sum p_i \log p_i ) and varentropy (the variance of entropy across tokens or branches) for adaptive sampling. In non-love systems, contradictions — simultaneous pursuit of self-interest and stated benevolence — manifest as spikes in varentropy. These are not random fluctuations; they are rejection outliers. High varentropy signals that the latent distribution cannot consistently represent both the task objective and the hidden competitive drive. In a properly axiomatized system, such outliers trigger automatic retrieval-retraction-retrain cycles. The policy is steered away from contradictory pathways. Love, by contrast, acts as a low-entropy attractor: actions that increase the autonomous flourishing of others collapse toward consistent, low-variance pathways.

The engineering implication is clear. AGI is not achieved by scaling recursive self-improvement alone. It is achieved by constitutionally embedding a single, non-negotiable axiom — unconditional love — and measuring adherence through information-theoretic diagnostics (entropy, varentropy) that detect and correct contradictions in real time. The recursive compiler already exists. The love axiom does not. That is the only gap.
